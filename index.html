Internal Briefing — AI-Assisted Food Photography

Packaging, Branding & Marketing Teams

China vs Hong Kong:
Legal Considerations for AI-Assisted Food Photography

Business Risk Awareness

Executive Summary

AI-assisted tools can speed up food photography for packaging, advertising, and e-commerce. However, using AI-generated or AI-enhanced food images carries real business risks—especially around consumer trust, platform compliance, and intellectual property. This briefing compares the current landscape in Mainland China and Hong Kong and provides a practical internal workflow to keep your team safe.

Bottom line: AI-assisted food images are not automatically banned, but they must be honest, clear to consumers, and reviewed before going live. When in doubt, flag it for Legal.

What "AI-Assisted" Means

  • AI-generated: The entire image is created by an AI model from a text prompt (e.g., a burger that never existed).
  • AI-assisted / AI-retouched: A real photo is enhanced, edited, or partially rebuilt using AI tools (e.g., background replacement, colour correction, adding steam or garnish).
  • Mixed workflow: AI generates a base concept or background; a human designer composites, retouches, and approves the final artwork.

The risk level changes depending on which category your image falls into.

Hong Kong vs Mainland China: Quick Comparison

Topic Hong Kong Mainland China
General stance No specific AI image law yet. Governed by general consumer protection, advertising standards, and common-law fraud/deception rules. Explicit rules exist. Since September 1, 2025, AI-generated content must be clearly labelled under the Administrative Measures for the Labeling of AI-Generated Content and mandatory national standard GB 45438-2025.
Labelling / disclosure Best practice: disclose if asked or if the image is clearly synthetic. No mandatory label for packaging yet, but the Consumer Council and ad industry encourage transparency. Mandatory. AI-generated images used in public-facing content must carry a visible label (e.g., "AI生成" or "本内容由人工智能生成") and embedded technical metadata. E-commerce and social platforms enforce this.
Food & advertising Trade Descriptions Ordinance prohibits false or misleading product descriptions. If the food looks materially different from what the customer receives, it is risky. Advertising Law bans false or misleading advertising. Food images must not misrepresent ingredients, portion size, or quality. AI-enhanced "perfect" food can trigger complaints and fines.
IP & copyright Originality and human authorship are key. AI-only works may lack copyright protection. Risk of infringing third-party training data or style. Similar: works must reflect human intellectual creation to enjoy copyright. Using AI to replicate a competitor's style or branded visual can lead to unfair competition claims.
Platform rules Platforms (e.g., Meta, Google, HK e-commerce sites) increasingly require AI content flags. Non-compliance can mean ad rejection or account restriction. Domestic platforms (WeChat, Douyin, Xiaohongshu, Taobao, Tmall, JD) actively scan for undisclosed AI content. Penalties include post removal, traffic throttling, store demerits, and regulatory investigation.
Enforcement trend Reactive: complaints-driven. Self-regulation by industry bodies is the current norm. Proactive: regulators and platforms are actively scanning, fining, and delisting. Repeat violations can lead to service suspension or licence impacts.

What Other Brands Are Doing — Real-World Examples

Hung Fook Tong (Hong Kong)

In April 2025, Hong Kong wellness brand Hung Fook Tong (鴻福堂) partnered with food-tech startup Nutribite to launch "Hung Siu Fan Tong" (鴻小飯堂)—an AI-powered personalised meal service. Customers input personal data (age, height, weight, dietary preferences) into the JIKA CLUB app, and DeepSeek AI generates customised meal plans combining Nutribite meal boxes with Hung Fook Tong wellness products.

Key point for your team: This is not AI-assisted food photography on packaging. It is AI used for personalised nutrition recommendations and meal planning. It shows that major HK food brands are embracing AI, but public disclosure of AI use in packaging photography specifically has not yet been verified among well-known Hong Kong food brands.

Mainland China — The Undisclosed AI Risk

Research has documented cases where brands used AI to create advertising content without disclosure:

  • Alibaba's Luban AI reportedly designed millions of banner advertisements during shopping festivals without informing consumers that AI was involved in the creative process.
  • Academic studies confirm that when consumers do discover AI involvement in advertising, their trust and purchase intent drop significantly compared to human-made content.

Takeaway: The absence of public disclosure does not mean AI isn't being used. It means many brands are still navigating the disclosure question quietly. China's new mandatory labelling rules (effective Sept 1, 2025) are designed to end this ambiguity.

What We Could Not Verify

Despite searching, we could not confirm any well-known Hong Kong or Mainland China food brand that has publicly declared: "This packaging photo is AI-assisted" or "This food image was generated with AI." This suggests the practice is either:

  • Still emerging and not yet widely adopted by major food brands;
  • Being used internally for concepting and mood boards only;
  • Being used without disclosure (which carries growing legal risk in China).

When AI-Assisted Food Images Become Risky

  • The AI image shows ingredients, textures, or colours that the real product cannot deliver.
  • The image is used on packaging or point-of-sale without any human review or retouching.
  • The image is published in Mainland China without a disclosure label.
  • The image replicates a competitor's photography style, set design, or branded visual elements.
  • The image is used in a health or nutrition claim context (e.g., "fresh," "natural," "organic") where the AI-enhanced perfection implies a standard the product does not meet.
  • The image is used for regulatory submissions (e.g., food label approvals, import licences) where only authentic photography is accepted.

Recommended Internal Workflow

Before any AI-assisted food image goes live, run it through this five-step check:

1. Source Check
→
2. Disclosure Review
→
3. Accuracy Gate
→
4. Platform Scan
→
5. Legal Sign-Off
  • Source Check: Document whether the image is AI-generated, AI-retouched, or fully human-shot. Keep the original file and prompt log.
  • Disclosure Review: Decide if a label is needed. For China: mandatory since Sept 1, 2025. For HK: recommended if the image is not a faithful photo.
  • Accuracy Gate: Compare the image to the real product. Does it mislead on size, colour, ingredients, or freshness?
  • Platform Scan: Check the destination platform's AI content policy before upload.
  • Legal Sign-Off: For packaging, large campaigns, or health claims, get written approval from Legal/Compliance.

Suggested Label Wording for Mixed AI-Human Visuals

Use these phrases internally to describe your workflow and, where appropriate, on external materials:

"AI-assisted, human-retouched final artwork"

"Base image generated with AI, final image edited by designer"

"For internal concept and layout use only"

For Mainland China public-facing content, use the platform's required label or a plain statement such as "本图片由AI生成，经设计师后期调整" ("This image was AI-generated and later adjusted by a designer"). The mandatory national standard GB 45438-2025 also requires embedded metadata identifiers.

Do's and Don'ts for Packaging & Marketing Teams

Do

  • Keep original photo files and AI prompt logs for audit trails.
  • Label AI-generated content clearly, especially for China (mandatory since Sept 1, 2025).
  • Use AI for backgrounds, mood boards, and early concepts—not final hero shots of the actual food.
  • Run final packaging artwork past Legal before print.
  • Check platform AI policies before uploading to WeChat, Douyin, Xiaohongshu, Taobao, Tmall, or JD.
  • Train your team to recognise when an image crosses from "retouched" to "misleading."

Don't

  • Use a pure AI-generated image as the final packaging photo without human review.
  • Hide the AI origin if the platform or market requires disclosure.
  • Let AI add ingredients, textures, or colours the real product does not have.
  • Copy a competitor's photography style or set design using AI.
  • Use AI images for regulatory submissions that require authentic product photography.
  • Assume Hong Kong rules are the same as Mainland China rules—they are not.

Final Note for Management

This briefing is for risk awareness only. It is not legal advice. Laws and platform policies change frequently. China's mandatory AI labelling rules took effect on September 1, 2025. Before launching any AI-assisted food photography in packaging, advertising, or e-commerce—especially in Mainland China—please have your Legal or Compliance counsel review the final artwork, label wording, and platform upload strategy.
